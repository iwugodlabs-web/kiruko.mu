# Fingerprint on the Kiosk — Plain-Language Explainer

Use this when talking to a customer (or a non-technical stakeholder) who asks
for fingerprint clock-in on the tablet. It explains why a tablet can't do it
on its own and lays out the options in everyday words.

---

## The short version

**A tablet's fingerprint scanner can only recognise the *owner* of the tablet
— not your workers.**

Think of a phone: your fingerprint unlocks *your* phone, but it can't tell the
phone who your friend is. A tablet works the same way. It's built to answer
"is this the owner?" — not "which employee is standing here right now?" So if
we want employees to clock in with a real fingerprint, the tablet can't do it
on its own. We'd need to attach a separate fingerprint reader.

---

## The options, in plain words

| # | Option | What it means | New hardware? | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Use the camera (recommended)** | The tablet already takes a photo at clock-in. Make that photo smart — compare it to the employee's photo and flag any mismatch for a manager to check. Catches "your friend clocked in for you". | No | Low |
| 2 | **Connect to a fingerprint clock the customer already owns** | If they already use a fingerprint time clock (e.g. ZKTeco), we pull its records into our system. They keep their device; we add the data. | No (theirs) | Low |
| 3 | **Buy a fingerprint reader for every tablet** | Attach a separate fingerprint reader. Slow, expensive, and only works on Android tablets — not iPads. | Yes | High |

---

## The one thing they should know

A fingerprint is permanent. If a password leaks, you change it. If a
fingerprint leaks, the person can't change their finger. That's a real risk we
would be taking on their behalf — which is exactly why most companies today
either use the camera, or plug into a fingerprint clock they already own.

---

## What we suggest

- **Start with the camera (option 1).** It's cheap, works on every tablet, and
  needs nothing extra.
- **If they insist on fingerprint, connect to a reader they already own
  (option 2)** rather than building one from scratch.

---

## If they still want fingerprint (for the record)

This is what "option 3" actually involves, so the customer can decide with
their eyes open:

- A USB fingerprint reader per tablet (~$50–150) plus the maker's software kit.
- iPads are out — no external fingerprint readers on iPadOS.
- Roughly 6 weeks of work.
- A fingerprint database is treated as sensitive personal data under Mauritius
  (Data Protection Act 2017) and Madagascar (Loi 2014-038) law — data residency,
  consent, and breach rules apply.
